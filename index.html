Act II / Small experiments

A choice can be a test.
It needn’t be a verdict.

My note on Designing Your Life keeps coming back to permission to experiment. A plan can be a hypothesis. A conversation, a class, or a small tool can tell you more than another month of trying to think your way to certainty.

That habit connects my reading notes to The Open Syllabus: 55 channels, eight learning tracks, five hours a week. Worldview takes the same curiosity into everyday choices, evidence, and reflection.

Ask a question. Try something small. Notice what changes.

Act III / What work taught me

What each job
taught me to ask.

Clinical data pipelines at Mayo Clinic taught me reliability. Screening more than 200 hedge fund profiles at SkyBridge taught me to combine numbers with judgment. At BlackRock, risk reporting and capital-flow dashboards made the story behind a number matter just as much as the calculation.

A recommendation to read Good to Great stayed with me. Today I’m a solutions engineer at Dasseti, translating between investors and due-diligence software. Independently, I use AI coding tools to turn clearly described research questions into Garage tools.
