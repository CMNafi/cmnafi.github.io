Chapter 01

Dinajpur

Bangladesh
25.6279° N, 88.6332° E 2000 – 2016

I grew up in Dinajpur, a quiet northern district in Bangladesh known for its ancient terracotta temples, lush lychee orchards, and endless monsoon rains. Long before I ever touched a production database or parsed an SEC filing, my world was built on mathematics competitions, notebook physics problems, and dusty street cricket pitch construction.

Cricket was our informal laboratory for probability. We played with electrical tape wrapped tightly around tennis balls to make them swing in humid air. Setting field placements when defending small totals on cramped neighborhood grounds taught me the fundamentals of risk asymmetry: you cannot protect every boundary, so you have to calculate where the batter is most likely to make an unforced mistake.

Late nights were spent solving olympiad geometry problems by kerosene lamps during power cuts, cultivating a stubborn affection for formal systems that could be rigorously verified from first principles.

"Before software, cricket and geometry taught me that constraints are not limitations—they are the rules that make tactical optimization possible."
Chapter 02

Changshu

Jiangsu, China
31.6539° N, 120.7522° E 2016 – 2018

At sixteen, my life experienced a sharp, irrevocable discontinuity. I was awarded a full scholarship to attend United World College (UWC) Changshu China, situated on an island in Kuncheng Lake, an hour northwest of Shanghai.

Overnight, I went from a provincial Bangladeshi town to living in dormitories alongside students from over a hundred countries—classmates who had survived refugee camps, children of diplomats, and self-taught programmers from Eastern Europe. In our economics and philosophy seminars, we debated whether global capital allocation models could ever be both Pareto-efficient and ethically defensible.

Living in the heart of the Yangtze River Delta during China's high-speed rail and mobile payment explosion made one thing starkly clear: infrastructure determines destiny. The physical and digital systems a society builds dictate what its citizens can imagine. That era laid the foundation for Worldview, an interactive atlas deconstructing how individuals navigate culture, psychology, and ethics.

"Moving to China at sixteen permanently cured me of intellectual parochialism. You quickly learn that every human system is an engineered choice, not a natural law."
Chapter 03

Decorah & Des Moines

Iowa, United States
43.3017° N, 91.7896° W 2018 – 2022

My collegiate journey brought me to the Upper Midwest to study Data Science, Mathematics, and Economics. In Iowa, the stark winters and quiet limestone bluffs provided an ideal environment for deep, uninterrupted technical study.

My undergraduate years became a sequence of high-velocity apprenticeships across health informatics and institutional finance:

  • Mayo Clinic (Rochester, MN): Engineered quantitative health data pipelines, modeling longitudinal patient cohort statistics and learning how high-stakes systems must maintain absolute data integrity under audit scrutiny.
  • SkyBridge Capital (Summer 2021): Immersed in fund-of-hedge-funds due diligence and multi-manager absolute return strategies. Between analyzing manager tracking errors, my mentor handed me Jim Collins' Good to Great, sparking a lifelong interest in how capital structures shape organizational discipline.
  • BlackRock: Explored institutional portfolio risk engines (Aladdin), factor attribution, and liquidity management, experiencing firsthand the computational scale needed to coordinate trillions of dollars of global institutional savings.
"Institutional finance is ultimately an exercise in data plumbing: whoever can turn messy, distributed regulatory disclosures into clean relational state wins."
Chapter 04

Tampa Bay

Florida, United States
27.9506° N, 82.4572° W 2022 – Present

Today, I live and work in the Tampa Bay region as a Solutions Engineer at Dasseti. We build the diligence and data infrastructure that institutional asset allocators, pensions, and fund managers rely on to evaluate private equity, private credit, and alternative funds.

On nights and weekends, I run the Editorial Systems Lab:

  • Building Regulatory Lens and ADV Screener: Parsing the complete SEC IARD universe (56,000+ registered advisers) out of raw XML bulk dumps into SQLite, orchestrated with FastMCP servers and fine-tuned LLM evaluators.
  • Writing long-form research monographs on corporate capital structures—such as tracing Uber’s $32.8B funding history or analyzing the 2025 cash flows of twelve major sports leagues.
  • Playing competitive league cricket across Tampa Bay fields (Waverly Park, Evans Park). Whether bowling a death-over spell defending 5 runs or hitting 78 off 47 in a tense chase, cricket remains the grounded, tactile counterpart to software architecture.
"Whether it's defending seven runs in the final over at Waverly or parsing 56,000 SEC adviser disclosures, the goal remains the same: clarity under pressure."
