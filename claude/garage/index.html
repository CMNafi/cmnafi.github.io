    Regulatory Lens

    Look up any SEC-registered investment adviser and see its registration, brochure and 13F holdings in one profile you can search or ask questions of.

    Key figure: 49,574 advisers · 6.75M 13F holdings

    Architecture

    1. SEC bulk files: ADV, 2A, 13F →
    2. Python ingest →
    3. SQLite, joined on CRD →
    4. FastMCP tools + TinyLlama →
    5. Search workspace
    Why I built it, and how it works

    Why I built it. Due diligence on an investment manager starts with public filings, and my work has always sat close to it: screening hedge funds at SkyBridge, and now software for diligence teams at Dasseti. But an adviser’s registration (Form ADV Part 1), brochure (Part 2A) and 13F holdings live in three separate SEC datasets, slow to pull together by hand and not structured for AI agents. I wanted them joined into one profile.

    How it works. A Python pipeline streams the SEC bulk files into SQLite, joins all three sources on the CRD number, and exposes the result through a seven-tool FastMCP server and a TinyLlama model fine-tuned with QLoRA on the brochures.

    • Astro
    • Python
    • SQLite
    • PostgreSQL
    • FastMCP
    • FastAPI
    • QLoRA / TinyLlama

Simple heuristic, not a model. How it’s computed

A simple heuristic, not a statistical model. From the striker we sweep 72 shot directions, weighted toward straight and leg-side hits (an assumption). A direction counts as guarded when a boundary rider stands within about 14° of it (fading to nothing by 32°) for boundary protection, or a ring fielder 8 to 36 yards from the bat stands within about 16° (fading by 34°) for single prevention.

Rules shown: standard T20 fielding restrictions (at most 2 fielders outside the 30-yard circle in the powerplay, overs 1 to 6; at most 5 after it) and Law 28.4 (no more than 2 fielders behind square on the leg side). League playing conditions, and shortened matches, can differ.
