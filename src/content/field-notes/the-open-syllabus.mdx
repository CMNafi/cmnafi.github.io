**The best university on earth has no course catalogue.**

YouTube holds more good teaching than any campus I have ever set foot in, and absolutely no way to find it. Most lists of channels worth watching, including [Deepak Mehta's](https://thelazygoan.substack.com/p/the-best-youtube-channels-for-lifelong), the one that sent me down this path, are indexes. This is my attempt at something else: not a list, a curriculum, with a time budget attached and an argument about what each track actually buys you.

Everything below is free. Nothing below is sponsored. Every channel is one I have watched enough of to defend.

## How to actually use this

**Pick two tracks, not eight.** The failure mode of a list like this is treating it as a queue. Take one track you need for work and one you have no business studying, and ignore the other six until those two feel boring.

**Watch one thing twice.** Grant Sanderson's linear algebra series repays a second pass more than nine new videos repay a first. Repetition is the whole difference between entertainment and a course.

**Write down the disagreement.** The moment you think a video is wrong is the only moment you are learning rather than absorbing. Note it, then go find the primary source. Half my blog posts started as an argument with a YouTuber.

**Let the algorithm off the leash carefully.** Subscribe, then watch from the subscription feed rather than the home page. The recommendation engine is optimised for your attention, not your education, and it is very good at its job.
