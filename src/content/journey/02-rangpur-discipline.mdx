At twelve I left home for Rangpur Cadet College, a military school. On a map it is sixty-six kilometres from Dinajpur. At twelve, it might as well have been another country.

It was the first room I had to learn by myself. Routine, repetition, hierarchy, accountability: the rules were written down. How the place actually worked was not, and nobody explains that part. You watch the people who have been there longer, you notice what they notice, and you adjust.

The structure was external at first. Somewhere along the way it moved inside. I learned to keep my own rhythm when the environment was demanding, to stay composed when things felt uncertain, and to keep showing up after the work stopped being exciting. Effort, it turns out, has a texture. Most of it is quiet.

I don't romanticize every hard place. But this one built the floor that everything else stands on.
