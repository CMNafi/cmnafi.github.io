Who runs the till, the pumps, and the back office, and where it breaks

Independent shops don’t run on one system. They run on a patchwork: a POS at the counter, a forecourt controller at the pumps, a price book in Excel, invoices on paper, and a distributor on the phone. This report maps that whole stack, smoke & vape, gas stations & c-stores, restaurants, liquor, grocery/deli, and boutiques, what each tool costs, how much its maker earns, what is still manual, and where AI can credibly take over.

Executive summary

The headline is not that retail lacks software. It’s that the software stops at the seams.

The winners are vertical, not horizontal

Toast owns independent restaurants. Block / Square is the broadest SMB platform (4M+ sellers, $250B GPV). Fiserv / Clover is largest by volume ($310B+ annualized GPV) but sells opaquely through ISOs and banks. PDI owns the independent c-store / fuel back office. Each wins inside one workflow, not across all of retail.

Payments is 10× the software

Toast monetises at ~98 bps of GPV. Its $2.05B ARR across 164K locations is ~$12.5K per location per year, but most of that is processing net revenue, not subscription. Any entrant pricing only SaaS is fighting for the smaller pool while incumbents defend the larger one with contracts and bundled hardware.

The manual wedges are identical everywhere

Distributor ordering by phone/text, paper invoice reconciliation, physical inventory counts, shift cash audits, Excel price books, and SOP binders appear in every vertical. They persist because no vendor owns “receiving → price book → reorder” end-to-end for independents.

AI is arriving at the edges, not the centre

Invoice digitisation, auto-POs, demand forecasting, AI marketing and ID scanning are already productised, mostly by specialists: Cigars POS, Bottle POS, FTx, Toast IQ, PDI’s PDIQ layer (2026). There is still no single cross-vertical AI back-office platform. That integration gap is the white space.

The one-sentence thesis:

Build the connective tissue independents still do by hand, invoices in, price book updated, reorder out, cash and shrink reconciled, processor-agnostic, without a 1–3 year lock-in, and you undercut Toast, Aloha and QuickBooks at once.

What’s different here

Case-break tracking (bottle / case / keg units), mix-and-match promos, distributor EDI / Fintech invoice integration, automated PO / receiving / reconciliation, age verification, and state reporting (TX wholesale, PA beer distributor).

Live migration wave

Heartland LiquorPOS is winding down, a migration wave is in progress (KORONA, 2026). Bottle POS auto-invoicing and FTx’s EDI add-on are the clearest examples of the invoice loop being productised in a single vertical.

Still manual: distributor rep ordering, invoice matching by hand, physical inventory, shelf-price maintenance.

Vendors: IT Retail ($49/mo), LOC SMS, KORONA, SuperSonic, NRS, Truno / Hobart scales, PDI (larger operators).

Deli-specific workflow

Scale integration with price-embedded barcodes, fresh-food shrink tracking, label printing, and EBT / SNAP acceptance. The scale PLU list is the price book, and it’s maintained by hand in most small stores.

Still manual

Fresh shrink logs, scale PLU maintenance, vendor credits, and prep lists on paper. No vendor in the research owns fresh-shrink + scale + POS as one loop for independents.

Vendors: Shopify POS (Basic $39/mo + POS Pro $89/mo/location; in-person 2.6% + 10¢), Lightspeed Retail (from $69/mo/register, note other Lightspeed tiers start at $109/mo for smoke-shop use in §1.1), Square Retail (Plus $89/mo/location, 2.5% + 10¢), Clover.

Still manual: purchase orders via email to reps, manual inventory counts, spreadsheet buy plans, Instagram / manual marketing. The gap is buying, not selling, the POS is solved, the buy plan isn’t.

5   Analysis A, Manual steps, ranked by AI automatability

★★★★★ = productisable today with OCR / LLM / time-series on data the shop already generates. Lower stars need cameras, physical counts, or human judgment that regulation won’t delegate.

The gap in one line:

Invoice-to-reorder and distributor EDI are productised only in pockets, liquor via Fintech, smoke via Cigars POS AI-PO. No cross-vertical vendor owns “receiving → price book → reorder” end-to-end for independents.

6   Analysis B, Disruption white space

Where incumbents are structurally weak, not just missing a feature.

01 · Business model

Lock-in is the product

Toast (1–3 yr terms, early-termination fees), Aloha Cloud (mandatory NCR processing), Clover ISO contracts (opaque, reseller-variable), FTx / Bottle $0 plans (locked to own processing). The #1 owner complaint pattern: pricing complexity, surprise fees (Toast PCI ~$9.95/mo, statement/batch fees), inability to shop processing.

02 · Biggest opening

“Toast for gas stations” doesn’t exist yet

The fuel / c-store stack is technologically ancient: Passport POS hardware still ships on Embedded POSReady 7, distributor-bundled quote-only pricing, no modern API ecosystem. A cloud-native, API-first forecourt + back-office suite is the clearest opening in the whole report.

03 · Pricing umbrella

QuickBooks, Toast, Aloha

QuickBooks: 6 straight annual increases (Plus $140/mo, Advanced $340/mo) justified by AI features, room for a retail-native ledger at $30–60/mo. Toast’s $69/terminal/mo + $50–185/mo add-on stack (≈$1–2K/mo all-in full-service) and Aloha’s $175/terminal/mo are the other umbrellas. KORONA’s processor-agnostic $59/mo proves “bring your own processing” works at SMB price points; Helcim’s interchange-plus model proves transparency sells.

04 · Breadth gap

Specialists win workflow, lose breadth

SuperSonic, Cigars POS, Bottle POS, mPower each nail one vertical’s receiving / rebate / EDI loop. None spans verticals; none offers a credible multi-vertical back office. Delivery-tablet chaos is only partially solved too, Otter (from $30/mo) aggregates orders, but menu-sync, fee reconciliation and marketplace P&L remain manual for most independents.

Top owner complaints (2026 review / comparison sources)

  • Contract lock-in & termination fees
  • Processing-rate opacity, especially Clover ISO variance
  • Per-terminal / per-location fee stacking (Toast $69/terminal, Aloha $175/terminal)
  • Support quality variance (POS Nation, Toast)
  • Hardware-first bundling; mandatory add-ons (KDS / kiosk per-device fees)

Why they don’t switch anyway

Menu / SKU catalog rebuild (thousands of SKUs), hardware investment ($3–10K/site), staff retraining, gift-card liability migration, loyalty member migration, processor contract overlap, and integration rewiring (accounting, payroll, delivery). Real switching clusters around lease renewals and new-store openings, time your entry to those moments.

Integration gaps & underserved verticals

Integration gaps

POS ↔ accounting still often needs a paid intermediary (e.g. Bottle POS QBO sync); distributor EDI is vertical-siloed; fuel POS doesn’t talk to a modern back office; age-verification logs don’t flow to audit / compliance reporting; loyalty data is fragmented across Toast / Square / Thanx / Punchh.

Underserved

Smoke/vape (high-risk processing 3–5%, FDA under-30 ID rule, scan-data rebates, only specialists serve it); independent c-stores (price book, DSD receiving, fuel reconciliation); liquor (distributor EDI, case-break); small grocery/deli (scale integration, SNAP/EBT, fresh shrink).
